# 7. Compulsion loop design, progression and prestige

Deep study. Evidence grades: **[S]** strong, **[M]** moderate, **[P]** practitioner, **[A]** our analysis.

---

## 7.1 Loop architecture
- **Nested loops** (*Sticky by Design*; standard design practice) [P]:
  1. **Moment-to-moment (seconds):** click, hit, run, collect. Must feel good on its own ([04](04-game-feel.md)).
  2. **Core loop (30 s – 5 min):** do, get, spend, improve. Example: plant, wait, harvest, sell, buy better seeds (Grow a Garden).
  3. **Session arc (10–30 min):** unlock a zone, finish a set, rebirth.
  4. **Meta arc (days to months):** collections, events, seasons, prestige.
  Each loop feeds the next. The core loop is what players repeat, and the meta arc is why they come back.
- **Close loops fast.** Hit games close a loop in under about 5 minutes and get several visits a day. Two 12-minute sessions count for more than one long one (*Built to Last*, developer analyses). Discovery weights play days across D1, D2–7 and D8–28 ([08](08-roblox-platform.md)). [M/S]
- **Hook model** (Eyal 2014): trigger, then action, variable reward, and investment. The investment (stored progress, a collection, a base) loads the next trigger. [P]
- **The core loop must be fun with the rewards taken away.** Tangible rewards reduce intrinsic interest when they feel controlling (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, 128 experiments). Avoid "log in and click claim" chores. [S]

## 7.2 Motivational levers, with evidence
| Lever | Evidence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal gradient.** Effort speeds up near a goal. | Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, *JMR* [S] | Progress bars everywhere, and next goals that are close and visible. |
| **Endowed progress.** A loyalty card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-filled was completed more often than a blank 8-stamp card. | Nunes & Drèze 2006, *JCR* [S] | Start new players partway through their first collection or quest chain. |
| **Zeigarnik effect.** Unfinished tasks stay on the mind. | Zeigarnik 1927 [M, mixed replication] | End sessions with a goal in view ("80% to the next island"). |
| **Fresh start effect.** People start goals more often after temporal landmarks (Mondays, month starts, birthdays). | Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, *Mgmt Sci* [S] | Weekly resets, seasons and "new season" moments re-engage lapsed players. |
| **Streaks.** At Duolingo, learners on a 7-day streak were **2.4x** more likely to return the next day. Separating the streak from the daily goal raised D14 retention by 3.3% and 7+-day streakers by over 40%. Simpler streak rules worked better. | Duolingo 2023 blog [M] | Simple streaks with a low bar (one round a day). Make them forgiving: freezes and repairs. |
| **Variable rewards.** | Ferster & Skinner 1957 [S] | Random drops in free play, plus guarantees (visible pity), see [02](02-gambling-monetisation.md). |
| **Flow.** Challenge just above skill. | Csikszentmihalyi 1990; Chen 2007 [S/P] | Self-selected difficulty tiers, and gentle easing after repeated failure. |

## 7.3 Return hooks ("something waiting tomorrow")
- **Offline growth.** Grow a Garden's crops grow while you're away, and Adopt Me! pets age, so logging in pays off immediately. This is the healthy D1 hook (*Built to Last*). [M]
- **Cap offline gains** (e.g. full after about 8 hours) so missing a day costs nothing. That keeps it a pleasant surprise rather than an obligation. [A, *Built to Last*]
- **Restocks and rotations.** Grow a Garden's seed shop restocks every few minutes and has weekly exclusives. These are shared moments ([06](06-social-virality.md)). [M]
- **Avoid negative return hooks.** Coming back to an emptied base (steal games) or losses for missed days ("your pet is sad") make returning feel like punishment. Players eventually stop. [M/A]
- **Weekly update cadence** gives novelty that doesn't rely on pressure. Brookhaven ships every Friday, and Grow a Garden staffed two event teams for weekly drops ([08](08-roblox-platform.md)). [M]

## 7.4 Progression design
- **Pacing:** fast early wins, then a gradually stretching curve. Too fast and content runs out. Too slow and players bounce. [P]
- **Tall vs wide** (*Built to Last*) [M]:
  - **Tall (a long ladder).** Blox Fruits has a level cap of 2,450, three Seas (a fourth due Dec 2026), Race V4, and Dungeon Mode. Its ceiling keeps moving.
  - **Wide (collections).** Adopt Me! has rarity tiers, ages, Neon (4 pets combined) and Mega-Neon (4 Neons combined). There's always another set to finish.
  - **No ceiling.** Brookhaven has no levels or currency. Players set their own goals.
  - **Simulator ladders that top out fast** (own the best item, rebirth a few times, then wait) are what fade.
- **Combine/merge steps** (e.g. 4 into 1 better) create long-term sinks and goals from duplicates. [P]
- **Multiple axes** (level, collection, cosmetics, rank, mastery) mean there's always something to chase, and different player types are served ([01](01-reward-neuroscience.md)). [P]
- **Plan expansions before launch** (the next "Sea" or island), and bank 4–6 weeks of content before launch (*Built to Last*). [P]

## 7.5 Prestige and rebirth
- **Rebirth:** reset progress for a permanent multiplier, currency or badge. It reuses content cheaply and gives veterans status (e.g. Steal a Brainrot's Rebirth). [P]
- **Make prestige visible:** titles, auras, nameplate stars and leaderboards. Status is especially motivating for teens ([01](01-reward-neuroscience.md)). [S/A]
- **Risk:** if rebirth just replays the same short ladder, the ceiling still arrives. Each prestige should unlock something *new* (an area, a mechanic, a cosmetic line). [A]

## 7.6 Healthy vs exploitative loops
| Healthy | Exploitative (avoid) |
|---|---|
| Fun without the timers. Timers only invite players back. | Fun only because of the timers, which means wanting without liking ([01](01-reward-neuroscience.md)). |
| Forgiving streaks (freeze, repair). | Streaks that wipe out when a child misses a day. |
| Capped offline gains. | Offline penalties and guilt prompts. |
| Natural stopping points (a run ends, a day/night cycle). | No stopping points, with leaving punished (the WHO lists gaming disorder in ICD-11). |
| Events that rotate back. | "Never coming back" pressure and must-be-online drops. |

## 7.7 Audit criteria
- [ ] Can the core loop be stated in one sentence, and is it fun without the rewards?
- [ ] Are there nested loops at the seconds, core (under 5 min), session and meta scales?
- [ ] Is there a positive reason to return tomorrow (capped offline growth, a restock, a daily) and no negative one?
- [ ] Are there reasons to return in a week (event, update) and in a month (season, expansion)?
- [ ] Are progress bars and the next goal always visible, with endowed progress for new players?
- [ ] Is the progression tall, wide, or both, and how far is the ceiling? When does a dedicated player run out?
- [ ] Is there a combine or merge sink for duplicates?
- [ ] Is there a prestige system that unlocks something new and shows status?
- [ ] Are streaks simple, forgiving and low-bar?
- [ ] Are there weekly or seasonal fresh-start moments?
- [ ] Are there natural stopping points, with no punishment for leaving?
- [ ] Is a weekly update cadence planned, with content banked?
